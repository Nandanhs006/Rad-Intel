Clinician review workspace

A small group of named clinicians review cases against one shared model and correct it where it is wrong. Access is issued by hand. There is no sign-up.

Read this before you log in

  • One shared model, pooled data. Every participating clinician reviews cases against the same model. Reviews from all sites are pooled and used to fine-tune that one model, which all participants then use. Your reviews are not kept to yourself, and neither is anyone else's.
  • What is stored. The radiograph you upload, its SHA-256 hash, your name and institution, the model's prediction and probability, your verdict, and any note. The image is kept on this server because training needs the pixels.
  • Who can see it. Every other logged-in clinician in this group, and the study administrator. It is not public and is not shared outside the group — but it is not private to you.
  • Your responsibility. Upload only images you are authorised to share, de-identified, with whatever approval your institution requires. Nothing here de-identifies an image for you.
  • One device at a time. Logging in here logs you out wherever else you were, and closing the browser logs you out. Each sign-in needs a fresh code, emailed to the address on your account.
  • What is logged. Logins, uploads, edits, deletions, exports and training runs are recorded against your name, and the administrator can read that log. It is what makes "everyone can see every case" accountable.
  • Scope. A research and education study. It does not provide diagnosis, treatment, triage or autonomous decision support, and it is not a medical device.
  • Leaving. Ask the administrator to disable your account at any time, and your cases can be deleted on request. Reviews already folded into a trained model cannot be retrospectively removed from that model's weights — that is why this is said before you log in and not afterwards.

Anything that needs sorting out — a lost Rad-Intel ID, a changed phone number, a case uploaded by mistake, a question about how your data is used — goes to the study administrator personally, using the contact details you were given when your account was issued.

What this system is

          

            Rad-Intel classifies paediatric chest radiographs as normal or pneumonia, shows
            which regions drove the score, and writes a structured summary from those recorded
            outputs. Classification, explanation and language generation are kept separate so
            that every displayed sentence traces back to a number the model produced.
          

          

            
            Restricted to technical research and education. It does not provide diagnosis,
            treatment, triage or autonomous decision support, and it is not a medical device.

Known limitations

          
    
            
  • Paediatric images from a single institution; nothing extends to other ages,
                institutions or acquisition protocols without external validation.
    • 
            
  • Labels collapse bacterial and viral pneumonia into one class.
    • 
            
  • No dependable patient identifier, so the split cannot guarantee patient-level
                independence.
    • 
            
  • Each configuration was trained once, so no result separates an architectural
                effect from seed variation.
    • 
            
  • Between 21% and 48% of model response falls outside the segmented lung fields,
                consistent with shortcut learning on this dataset.
    • 
            
  • Grad-CAM and LIME agree only weakly with each other; a heatmap shows model
                sensitivity, not pathology.
    • 
            
  • Generated summaries read as diagnostic to a reviewing clinician in half of
                sampled cases despite passing schema validation.
